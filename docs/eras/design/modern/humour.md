# Modern humour bible (binding for COMEDY-MOD and the COMEDY-EDITOR)

Owner: DESIGN-ERA-MODERN part 2. Companion files: `feel_sheet.md` s7 (engines named there, arrival card, arc), `rosters.md` (unit jokes, bark roles), `missions_outline.md` (briefings, set-piece lines, star text, rewards), `god_powers.md` (tooltips and the intern gag), `first_three_minutes.md` (beats and toasts). The Ancient bureaucratic understatement is NOT primary here: this era's comedy is loud technical difficulty (E1), household objects taken at face value (E2) and things that are late, lost or in the wrong lane (E3). Forms, stamps and clipboards are chrome and a faction's lore, not the commentators' engine: at most one line in five may be about paperwork.

Voices (unchanged across eras, spec/humor.md s2): **Brutus** (play-by-play; bombastic; one ALL-CAPS emphasis word per line, a place or title may add a second; the radio-procedure gag), **Plato** (colour; rhetorical questions; undercuts Brutus in six words; ends on a question or a measured understatement; "PLATO (MUTED)" in M1 to M8), **Cassandra** (analyst; short flat sentences; "I said"; right and ignored; has muted herself on purpose). The commentators have been dragged through time by Zeus's intern, who stays offstage.

Tone law: jokes punch at situations, hubris, equipment, hats, teapots, lunchboxes, headsets and the three commentators; never at people, press, soldiers or any real force. Deaths are comic: units are **knocked out** (hat pops, dazed ring, sit, fade), machines pop a lid and sigh steam; no pleading, family, pain or blood. Pins are a pushpin, never a wound. Spin and briefing jokes target the euphemism, never a real spokesperson or a real event.

## 1. Three comedic engines

### E1 Live From The Front (Technical Difficulties) (headsets, call signs, hold music, mute buttons, spin, live-broadcast mishaps; Brutus-led, loud, overt)

Rules: (1) the war is a broadcast and every disaster is a technical one first: a mute, an echo, the wrong channel, a delay, hold music; (2) the failure is never cruel; (3) Brutus treats radio procedure as tactics ("Over", "Copy", "Roger") but at most one radio-procedure word per line and at most one such line in three; (4) Plato's lines arrive muted (glyph and label) and he keeps talking; Cassandra has muted herself on purpose and speaks only when it matters; (5) spin ("proactive relocation", "temporarily disassembled") is the euphemism joke, aimed at the phrase and not at a person.

1. [B, battle_start] "This is BRUTUS, call sign Brutus! I picked it myself! Over! Is it over? Over!"
2. [B, battle_start] "I am receiving you loud and... I am receiving MYSELF. Hello, myself!"
3. [P, battle_start, muted] "Over. Out. Roger. Three ways of saying nothing in a hurry."
4. [C, battle_start] "Channel four is the enemy's. I said channel four. Brutus has been on channel four since noon."
5. [B, kill_streak] "FIVE in a row, and the headset says I am on mute! Can anybody hear a streak?"
6. [C, army_low] "Official statement: the position held. Unofficial: the position, the road and the briefing room."
7. [B, retreat] "A bold new RETREAT strategy! The briefing calls it proactive relocation! The troops call it running!"
8. [P, stalemate, muted] "Both armies are on hold. Whose call is the more important?"
9. [P, defeat, muted] "'A minor setback.' The setback has a crater and a name. Is it still minor if it has a name?"
10. [B, victory] "We are live from the front and we WON! Technical difficulties! We won though! Over!"

### E2 Toy-Box Literalism (a war fought with household objects and narrated with total seriousness; "what is it for?"; Plato-led)

Rules: (1) the object is taken at face value: a teapot is a teapot, a lunchbox is a lunchbox, and the weapon is the surprise; (2) the narration never says "toy"; it says "tank" with a straight face; (3) Plato asks what the object is for; (4) specific nouns beat adjectives (biscuit, lid, drawer, handle, plywood, hose, sprig); (5) the object's logic is the target, never the person inside it.

1. [P, teapot_heavy fires] "The Teapot Heavy has not poured. It has only aimed. I find that more menacing."
2. [B, peashooter hit] "The flowerpot takes a light hit! The sprig is FINE! I repeat, the sprig is fine!"
3. [C, sandbag destroyed] "The sandbags are sand and bag. Bag was the weak link. I said bag."
4. [B, filing_howitzer fires] "The Filing-Cabinet Howitzer opens drawer THREE! A shell! Of course it is! Drawer four is lunch!"
5. [P, trolley_rammer charges] "A trolley with a plank. We have reinvented the ram, with a wobblier wheel and fewer principles."
6. [B, biscuit_tank] "The Biscuit-Tin Tank pops its LID! The good biscuits are at the back! So is the armour!"
7. [C, lunchbox_apc bailout] "The crew bailed out of the lunchbox. They are holding sandwiches. No sandwich was dropped. I checked."
8. [P, hoarding_bearer] "A plywood wall with legs. Is it protecting him, or merely introducing the bullets to the sign?"
9. [B, fishbowl_chopper] "The goldfish presses its face to the GLASS! Rockets away! The fish has more flight hours than the pilot!"
10. [C, pin_applied] "The pushpin is a pushpin. It does what pushpins do. I am surprised nobody has put one on me."

### E3 The Convoy Is Late (logistics slapstick: traffic, detours, wrong exits, cows, parking, deliveries, refuelling; Cassandra-led doom about mundane delays)

Rules: (1) things are late, lost, in the wrong lane or at the wrong exit, and the war waits politely; (2) numbers are small and specific (four minutes, level three, the third exit); (3) Cassandra predicts the delay and is ignored; (4) the roadworks cow has right of way, always; (5) the delay is never cruel: a column is stuck, a fuel truck is late, a form is on the wrong desk.

1. [B, escort] "A tank column stuck behind a COW! The cow has right of way! The cow has always had right of way!"
2. [C, escort] "The convoy will take the wrong exit. The third time. I drew them a map. They used it as a coaster."
3. [P, stalemate, muted] "The fuel truck is late. The war is therefore postponed, or wins by default."
4. [B, battle_start, roadworks] "PARKING STRUCTURE! Level three is free! Level three is also the battle!"
5. [C, wave] "The reinforcements are four minutes away. Four minutes ago they were four minutes away."
6. [P, bridge, muted] "One bridge, one column, and the detour is also a bridge. Is the detour the more honest bridge?"
7. [B, boxcar] "The wagons were due TUESDAY! It is Thursday! The cargo is celebrating anyway!"
8. [C, mine_armed] "The sappers marked the minefield with tape. The tape arrived after the minefield. I said order of delivery."
9. [P, convoy, muted] "A column of tanks waits for a signal. The signal waits for a column. Who proposed the arrangement?"
10. [B, helicopter_flyover] "The helicopter is LATE! Its risk assessment, I am told, is on time!"

## 2. Per-surface style guides

Every guide gives the rule, the limit, a good example and one thing to avoid. Limits match spec/humor.md (bubbles <= 12 words, announcer <= 22 words, tips <= 18 words).

| surface | rule | limit | example | avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **unit blurb** (codex, hover) | what the unit DOES in the rules, then one joke that names the mechanic; present tense | <= 14 words | Tin-Hat Trooper: "Fires two-round bursts from eight rounds and reloads behind a friend's biscuits." (names the magazine) | adjectives with no mechanic ("brave, loyal") |
| **unit lore** (codex) | the faction joke, one real number allowed | <= 35 words | Directorate: "Exists to supply the war and has since come to believe it is the war. Every shell is filed before it is fired and archived after it lands." | real places, real forces, real kit |
| **death line** (last words) | 3 or more per unit; kit, hats, lids, steam, biscuits; units are knocked out; the unit never begs | <= 12 words | Tin-Hat Trooper: "My hat! Somebody get my hat!" / Teapot Heavy: "Tea is... served." / Hoarding Bearer: "Please read the sign. The sign says stop." | pleading, family, pain, "tell my wife" |
| **taunt** | an in-character brag or complaint, 2 or more | <= 10 words | Clerk Rifleman: "Reloading in triplicate. Please wait." | insults aimed at the player's real traits |
| **announcer line** | one beat, caps on exactly one word for Brutus; slots allowed ({unit}, {n}, {arena}); once-per-battle where it is a set piece | <= 22 words | [P] "A crater is a decision that is too late to reverse." | meta jokes about the interface; two jokes in one line; more than one radio-procedure word |
| **tip** | half true hints (counters, controls, rules), half jokes that also teach; every claim true of the rules at tuning | <= 18 words | "Cover halves hits from the front only. A flanked unit loses it." | balance numbers BALANCE may change |
| **briefing line** | three voices, one line each; Brutus names the setting, Plato asks, Cassandra warns | <= 30 words | see `missions_outline.md` | spoilers for the star condition |
| **results lesson** (Cassandra) | a true statement of what happened, with {n}; the fix is a plain second line | <= 20 words + fix <= 14 | "{n} of ours were caught reloading in the open. A magazine is a promise." / fix: "Reload behind a friend, or behind a hedge." | claims that can be false (padding lessons never print {n}) |
| **kill verb** | transitive phrase that takes the victim; 6 or more per cause; environmental causes also get 3 `solo` phrases | 1 to 4 words | explosive: "shelled", "filed under craters" | gore; anything that sounds painful |
| **wave name** | a nuisance or social event, plural or "of", Title Case | <= 5 words | "A Meeting, Armed" | the Ancient and Medieval names; jokes about real people |
| **loading line** | present participle ending in "...", an offstage task for a ridiculous object | <= 10 words | "Teaching a tank which end is the front..." | full sentences, questions |

## 9. Banned and allowed word notes for this era (feeds the text sweep, ER11, with a signed allowlist)

Banned in any player-visible string (case-insensitive, whole word unless stated): real nations, forces and services (NATO, army or navy or air force as a named service, marines as an organisation, SWAT, CIA, KGB, SAS, special forces); real wars, battles and campaigns (World War, D-Day, Normandy, Stalingrad, Vietnam, Korea, Gulf, Iraq, Afghanistan, Blitzkrieg, Pearl Harbor, Hiroshima); extremist and terror vocabulary (terrorist, hostage, suicide, jihad, insurgent, massacre, genocide, atrocity, war crime, ethnic cleansing); real weapon, vehicle and aircraft brands, designations and nicknames (AK, M16, Kalashnikov, Glock, Uzi, Humvee, Hummer, Jeep, Abrams, Sherman, Tiger, Panzer, Apache, Black Hawk, Chinook, Huey, Spitfire, Stuka, Tomahawk, Patriot, HIMARS, Nerf, Lego); real franchise terms (space marine, Warhammer, Call of Duty, Battlefield); real flags, anthems and national colour sets; real medical or protected emblems (red cross, red crescent); nuclear and chemical weapon words (nuke, mushroom cloud, napalm, nerve gas); pain and injury vocabulary that describes suffering (bleeding, screaming, agony, gore, wound, corpse); "kill" stays allowed as an objective and kill-feed word, never in a first-person plea; slurs, ethnicity and religion as a punchline; any real person's name, including pilots, generals and politicians.

Allowed (the sweep must not flag these; each is setting or generic English): rifle, pistol, machine gun, MG, tripod, sniper, sharpshooter, cannon, howitzer, mortar, shell, shells, artillery, barrage, bullet, round, ammo, magazine, reload, burst, trench, bunker, dugout, sandbag, mine, minefield, grenade (generic), dynamite, tank, armour, helicopter, gunship, drone (toy), balloon, parasol, umbrella, radio, headset, squelch, hold music, briefing, requisition, convoy, column, dozer, plough, trolley, runabout, lunchbox, teapot, filing cabinet, mower, wheelbarrow, drainpipe, knocked out, pinned, flank, cover, hedge, hay bale, picnic, barge, harbour, dam, airfield, runway, control tower, sluice, boxcar, rail yard, roadworks, cone, cow (right of way), goldfish, biscuit, sandwich, clipboard, stamp, memo, parcel, intern (offstage), "Over", "Out", "Roger", "Copy". The words "casualties" and "unplanned rapid redeployments" appear only in the Briefing Brigade's lore joke. Heraldic and hazard colours are allowed as jokes about stationery only when no real flag is described.

Tone checks the reviewer pass applies to every line: (1) does it punch at a situation or an object, not a person or a real force; (2) could a nation, force or faith recognise itself; (3) is any death line asking for sympathy or describing pain; (4) is "pin", "mine" or "shell" attached to suffering (it must read as a toy-box event); (5) is the line copied from the Ancient or Medieval pool (Jaccard >= 0.6 is a near-duplicate); (6) does Brutus have exactly one ALL-CAPS emphasis word besides names and places; (7) does Plato end on a question or a measured understatement; (8) is more than one line in five about paperwork (E1-E3 are not Ancient's engine).
