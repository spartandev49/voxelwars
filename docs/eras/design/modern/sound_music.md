# Modern sound and music bible (binding for AUDIO and HUNTER; `spec/AU` takes its rows from here)

Owner: DESIGN-ERA-MODERN part 2. Consumers: AUDIO (AU0 sound bible, router tables, music slots, stingers), HUNTER (source matrix), COMEDY-MOD (bark sound hooks). The palette is fixed by `feel_sheet.md` s5 (poppy, clacky, crackly); this file adds the sources, variant counts, loop and mix rules. All third-party material is CC0, CC BY 3.0/4.0 or public domain; CC BY-SA, GPL and NC are rejected (plan s10). Every cue has a synth fallback in `synth.js`. Hosts that work: OpenGameArt, Kenney, incompetech, archive.org, SoundBible; freesound, pixabay, zapsplat and musopen are blocked; Commons is throttled. **Nobody has heard any candidate**: every pick below is a search result judged by metadata (title, bpm, feel, instruments), and the verification report says so.

## 1. Sound bible (AU0)

**Three adjectives: poppy, clacky, crackly.** Guns are cap-gun pops with a cork-and-tin edge; every mechanical thing is a clack (typewriter, stapler, stamp, bolt, latch); every voice and every engine bed arrives with a radio crackle.

| aspect | rule |
|---|---|
| reference timbres | cap-gun and toy-pistol pops, popped balloons and paper bags, a tin biscuit lid dropped on a table, a typewriter carriage return, a stapler, a rubber stamp on a pad, a bolt clack, a pen click, a wooden ruler slapped on a desk; radio squelch and a half-second of static; a kettle whistle; vibraphone, upright bass, brushed snare, baritone sax, muted trombone, organ swell; a toy engine's sewing-machine clatter; a phone-line click; a lawn-mower pull-cord |
| forbidden timbres | movie-grade gunshots with long low tails; a film "boom" and any trailer braam or hybrid-orchestral hit; real siren recordings (the stalemate siren is a toy siren synth); screams and anything that reads as pain; real radio-chatter recordings or any speech that sounds like footage; a modern cinematic drum-and-drone score; patriotic anthems or march pieces that evoke a real force; bugles playing real calls (the "wrong bugle" is a flat toy bugle); real emergency tones |
| realism processing | **applied**: gunfire is cut to 0.15 to 0.35 s pops, tails shortened to a quarter of the source, less low end (high-pass at 160 Hz on small arms, 80 Hz on cannons); explosions are a balloon pop plus a cymbal tail, a fuller whoomp for medium and a cartoon boom with a crater thud for large, never a film explosion; the master chain (compressor, limiter, soft clip) is unchanged |
| loudness classes (short-term LUFS targets, `mixtest` asserts) | `pop` (rifle, pea, SMG) -20; `burst` (MG) -21; `crack` (sharpshooter) -17; `snap` (near miss) -22 but closer to the camera; `clack` (reload) -24 and audible at 35 u; `thump` (cannon, mortar, howitzer) -16 with a 4 dB music duck; `whistle` (shell) -20; `blast` small -18, medium -16, large -14; `tink` (armour front) -17, the loudest small thing in the game; `bed` (engine, tread, rotor) -26; `ui` -24; commentators -14 after the squelch |
| voices | the engine budget (32 voices, 24 on Potato, 8 reserved for ambience) is unchanged; the Modern hot families need `burst` templates (cooldown and `maxVoices` per family below); far sounds thin above 0.65 load; the gunfire is an aggregate bed above a threshold of shooters in frustum (below) |
| UI sounds | hover: a stapler tick; click: a rubber-stamp thud; Deploy and confirm: stamp plus a typewriter ding; star awarded: three stamps in a row, each a little higher; cooldown ready: a pen click; overlays: paper rustle; the radio squelch before every commentator line |

Working titles are from `feel_sheet.md` s5; the real file titles go in the credits. ## 4. Twelve hot sound families

Hot families get at least 6 independent sources (the plan's rule); heavy singles at least 3. "Reuse" names existing ledger rows from the Ancient release; the Ancient release has no firearm, so almost everything is new. Cue ids carry `mod_`; the router gets a `PROJ_AUDIO` / `EXPLOSION_AUDIO` / `ABILITY_CUE` row per family (AU4). Total new Modern rows are budgeted at 120 (plan s1). The twelve are the thirteen of `feel_sheet.md` s5 with the engine, tread and rotor beds merged into one aggregate family (family 11), because one bed with three layers is one router entry.

No siren, no real traffic or crowd recording that sounds like footage; HUNTER searches OGA (CC0) for each family, none is verified.

## 6. Router and mix notes for AUDIO

1. Every Modern def sets `audio` profile keys (hit, swing, shoot, death, voice, step) through the planned `AUDIO_PROFILES` table; the dead `UnitDef.sfx` hook is not used. The step table is keyed by the arena material (`concrete`, `asphalt`, `paving`, `ballast`, `steel_deck`, `lino_tile`).
2. New event handlers: `pin_applied` (thunk and the ring tick), `bullet_snap` (near-miss, camera-distance gated), `reload_start` (clack), `reload_hit`, `cover_hit` (a soft pad thud), `armour_plink` and `armour_flank_hit` (tink vs bonk), `first_indirect` and `telegraph` (the shell whistle exactly 2.2 s long), `setup`, `bailout` (lid pop), `crater`, `mine_armed` and `hazard_trigger`, `air_spawn`, `unit_heal`, `script_beat`, `setpiece` (the single dispatcher fans out stinger, sfx and announcer duck).
3. A set-piece plays its sfx list at the shot's start; the stinger ducks the music; the announcer line is queued after the stinger's first beat (priority 5, bypass alternation); the radio squelch precedes every commentator line, not the sfx.
4. `mixtest` per era runs three scenarios: a 150-unit firefight at the hedgerow with pins, reloads and the hamper; a tank column on the bridge with the dozer set-piece and the armour tinks; the dam finale with the Chandelier, the Teapot and the Final Notice. Per-family loudness classes (section 1) are asserted; true peak < -1 dBFS; no clipping; hot-family repetition (the same variant twice in a row) < 15 percent; the gunfire bed does not exceed the music by more than 3 dB at the loudest second.
5. Not verifiable here: the sound by ear, the real-device mix, whether the candidate tracks fit the tempo targets, and whether a pop reads as a pop and not as a firework; the verification report says so.
