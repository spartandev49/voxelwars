# Sci-Fi sound and music bible (binding for AUDIO and HUNTER; `spec/AU` takes its rows from here)

Owner: DESIGN-ERA-SCI-FI part 2. Consumers: AUDIO (AU0 sound bible, router tables, music slots, stingers), HUNTER (source matrix), COMEDY-SF (bark sound hooks). The palette is fixed by `feel_sheet.md` s6 (glassy, humming, squelchy, with a heavy thump for mechs and the orbital strike); this file adds sources, variant counts, loop and mix rules. All third-party material is CC0, CC BY 3.0/4.0 or public domain; CC BY-SA, GPL and NC are rejected (plan s10; several packs on disk list SA or GPL alongside CC BY, and the CC BY option is the one taken, recorded per row). Every cue has a synth fallback in `synth.js`. Hosts that work: OpenGameArt, Kenney, incompetech, archive.org, SoundBible; freesound, pixabay, zapsplat and musopen are blocked; Commons is throttled. **Nobody has heard any candidate**: every pick below is a catalogue or metadata judgement (title, bpm, feel, instruments, licence page), and the verification report says so. "On disk" means the pack is already in `assets/raw/` with a `meta.json`; "hunt" means HUNTER must fetch or search and nothing is claimed about it.

Cue ids carry `sf_`. The mission and set-piece lists in `missions_outline.md` use the family names of `arenas.md` s3.2 (`dome_crack`, `emp_burst`, `mech_step_boom`, ...); the router cue id is always `sf_` + that name (`sf_dome_crack`).

## 1. Sound bible (AU0)

**Three adjectives: glassy, humming, squelchy.** Shields are glassy (a bloop-tink per hit with the pitch falling as the bubble empties, a shatter on break, a rising tick while it recharges); machines, hover and the world are humming (a turbine whine over a sub, a servo, the "pdddt" of a power-down); goo, acid and spores are squelchy (wet clicks, a happy "splorp", a chitter chorus). Mechs and the orbital strike add a fourth colour, a heavy thump, but never a boom.

| aspect | rule |
|---|---|
| reference timbres | glass tapped with a fingernail, a wet finger on a wine glass, a soap bubble pop, a tape-stop, a fridge compressor, a CRT warming up, a theremin wobble, a vibraphone, a vocoder hum, analog arps and bleeps, a toy laser, a bicycle horn, a squeak toy, a slide whistle, a hotel bell, a kettle starting, a rubber stamp, a dial-up modem chirp, a wet sponge squeeze, a fluorescent tube starting, a shopping-cart wheel |
| forbidden timbres | orchestral trailer brass and any trailer braam or hybrid-orchestral hit (`fanfare-for-space` and `exciting-trailer` are on disk and NOT used); real gunshot recordings; any swung-blade or humming-sword whoosh (no humming swords); real sirens (the klaxon is a toy two-tone synth); screams and anything that reads as pain; real speech or footage-like radio chatter; real emergency tones; a space-agency countdown voice (the robotic countdown on disk is a CC0 novelty read of numbers and is allowed only as a chopped, pitched tick, never as a mission-control voice) |
| realism processing | **applied**: Sci-Fi guns are chunky toy-box "pew" with a body thump, cut to 0.15 to 0.30 s, tails shortened to a quarter of the source, no real low end (high-pass 140 Hz on small arms, 70 Hz on cannons and mortars); rail and orbital are the long ones (rail 1.2 s ringing tail, orbital 2 s charge); explosions are a soap-bubble pop plus a glass tail (small), a fuller thoomp (medium) and a cartoon boom with a crater thud for large, never a film explosion; the master chain (compressor, limiter, soft clip) is unchanged |
| loudness classes (short-term LUFS targets, `mixtest` asserts) | `pew` (pulse, valet beam) -20; `chatter` (rivet gun) -21; `crack` (rail) -17; `lob` (plasma, glob, salvo launch) -19; `absorb` (shield hit) -24 and thinned; `pop` (shield break) -18, the most satisfying small sound in the era; `tick` (shield recharge) -26 but audible at 25 u; `emp` -16; `blink` -22; `shimmer` (cloak) -24; `thump` (mech step) -15 with a 4 dB music duck; `orbital` -14 with a 6 dB duck; `bed` (hover, machines) -26; `goo` -20; `blast` small -18, medium -16, large -14; `ui` -24; commentators -14 after the booth chirp |
| voices | the engine budget (32 voices, 24 on Potato, 8 reserved for ambience) is unchanged; Sci-Fi hot families need `burst` templates (cooldown and `maxVoices` per family below); far sounds thin above 0.65 load; shield absorb is rate-limited at the source (`shield_hit` <= 4 per unit per second, M.md s3.12) and again at the mixer (<= 3 per 100 ms in the frustum); a **laser bed** replaces individual pulse sounds above 40 shooters in the frustum |
| UI sounds | hover: a soft "tick" (Kenney interface, on disk); click: a glassy "bwip"; Deploy and confirm: a rising two-note "ting" (Kenney digital `twoTone`/`threeTone`, on disk); star awarded: three rising "ting"s, each a third higher; cooldown ready: a soft chime plus the sweep closing; overlays: a hex whoosh; the **booth chirp** (a 90 ms "bwip-ip" of a drive-in speaker clicking on) precedes every commentator line |
