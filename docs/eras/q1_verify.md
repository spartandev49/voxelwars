## BLOCKER

### Q1. G1 uses `stateHash`, which is blind to most of the game and which the plan itself says it will change
**Question.** Which fields does the Ancient golden actually hash, and what happens to the recorded goldens when "stateHash is extended to cover the new state" (non-negotiable 3, M3)?
**Evidence.** `src/sim/world.js:899-905`: the hash mixes only `u.id, u.x, u.z, u.hp, u.team`, `rng.s` and `tickN`. Not hashed: projectiles, effects, terrain (craters), props (gate hp, Troy walls), statuses, cooldowns, ability state, kills/stats, events, god powers, waves. `Math.fround(NaN)*1000|0` and the same for `Infinity` are `0` (probed), so a NaN position hashes like x=0. Plan G1 says "per-100-tick stateHash + final tally"; plan M3 says the hash is extended with `u.sup, u.sh, u.cloak, u.ammo, u.aim, u.layer`. Any change to what is mixed changes every number recorded "BEFORE any engine edit".
**Failure scenario.** (a) The extension lands, all six goldens flip, someone re-records them "because the hash function changed", and the proof that Ancient is unchanged is silently replaced. (b) A change to crater timing, prop damage, ability cooldown, event emission, or a NaN in a new field leaves the 5-field hash identical, G1 stays green, and the Ancient game differs (the audio and view run on events, which are not hashed at all).
**Required plan change.** Record G1 with TWO hashes: `legacyHash` (the exact current function, frozen forever, never extended) and `fullHash` (new function: props hp, craters, projectiles, effects, stats, kills, cooldowns, status slots, NaN-aware via `Object.is`/a NaN sentinel) plus an event-stream hash (type, tick, ids). New fields contribute exactly 0 bytes when unused, and a test asserts that for Ancient defs `fullHash` is a pure function of the old state. Add a guard that throws on non-finite unit state in test builds. State in plan section 3 that G1 may never be re-recorded without a diff-review entry in `docs/eras/golden_log.md` (what changed, why, who approved).

### Q2. Non-negotiable 1 ("Ancient does not change") contradicts planned Ancient-path fixes, and there is no change-control policy for a red golden
**Question.** When a planned engine fix necessarily changes Ancient battles, which rule wins: the golden or the fix?
**Evidence.** Plan M10: "`_tickEffects` buffer aliasing fix"; M12: "defenders stop breaching their own gates", "prop-death coalescing per tick" (Troy is one of the six G1 arenas); M2: "replace kind special cases" (`noLead/descentOnly/stick`); M7: air skipped by `_separate`, per-class flow fields; M13: ability registry cap 32 to 64; M15: `env.gravity` at 3 sites; M16: watchdog tuned. Each touches code that Ancient battles run. Section 0 item 1 says "ids frozen, stats frozen, layouts frozen" and e.md O4 says "Ancient campaign records must stay valid or be re-recorded deliberately". Nothing says how that decision is made.
**Failure scenario.** The Troy gate fix flips G1 troy; the SIM agent, who owns both the fix and the test, re-records G1 to get green. Ancient plays differently (gates, craters, retargeting), the 536 stored campaign battles are stale, and nobody notices because the golden that was supposed to notice was rebased by the author of the change.
**Required plan change.** Split changes into (a) "must be bit-identical for Ancient" (the default) and (b) "intentional Ancient delta", the latter listed up front in the plan (aliasing fix, own-gate rule) with the expected observable change and a re-record by someone other than the author, plus a before/after replay of the 9 Ancient missions and 6 puzzle solutions showing the same outcomes. Any fix not on the list must be gated behind "def uses it" (the plan's own principle) or be dropped. The re-record step needs two signatures in `golden_log.md`; the author of the sim change cannot be one of them.

### Q5. e.md criterion 1 (play every mission 1 to 9 through the real UI, "ends, awards stars, unlocks the next") is not what `modes.mjs` does, and V6 describes capabilities that do not exist
**Question.** Which tool actually plays 27 new missions to their end through clickable UI, and what does it cost?
**Evidence.** `tools/modes.mjs` sections: `campaign` plays only mission 1 by briefing and ends it with `world.step` slices; `missions` (lines ~83-101) calls `goto('briefing', ...)` (not the map or the era chooser), deploys with `autoFill`/`suggestArmy`/the stored puzzle solution, steps 600 ticks (20 sim-s) and asserts only `after.tick >= 300`. It does not wait for the end, check stars, check the unlock, or click NEXT. The survival section "wipes the enemy" with `w.removeUnit` to reach the intermission ("wiring check, not a balance check"). Plan V6: "every mission and puzzle of every era through the real build (deploy, army, run, end, stars, unlock, next)". Map 08 seam 14 only extends it to loop eras and still uses 20 sim-s.
**Failure scenario.** A mission whose win condition never fires in the real UI (an unreachable `capture` point, an `escort` that never completes, a `defend_core` that cannot lose) passes `modes`, because nothing ever waits for `ended`. The user finds it on mission 4 of Medieval.
**Required plan change.** Define V6 as two tools. (1) `modes --depth=smoke` (today's behaviour, per-era, fast). (2) `campaign_play --era=X` which for each of the 9 missions: opens via era chooser, map pin, Deploy (real clicks), places the authored reference army (`mission.reference`, mandatory for every new mission), fights to `ended` with a sim-time cap of the mission time limit, asserts the winner equals what feasibility recorded for that bot and seed, asserts stars written to progress, the next pin unlocked, the NEXT MISSION button navigates to the right briefing, and Back from every screen on the path returns to the era's map. Budget it explicitly: 27 missions x about 30 to 60 s of SwiftShader = 15 to 30 min total, run once per milestone under the heavy lock, plus once on the final build. Same for every puzzle (stored solution through the UI) and survival without removing units (let it play to wave 3 with a fixed army).

### Q6. "Counters are real" (e.md section 5, plan V4) is to be shown with 20-seed bots; the statistics cannot tell a counter from a coin flip
**Question.** What sample size and decision rule turn "pikes beat knights beat archers, AT beats tanks, EMP beats mechs, cloak beats range" into a pass/fail?
**Evidence.** Plan V4: "counters table ... measured with 20-seed bots". The verification report itself notes 20 seeds give "about 10 points of error" (that is one standard error: sqrt(.25/20) = 11 points; a 95% interval at n=20 is +/- 22 points; an observed 60% is consistent with 39% to 78%). Existing bands: U5b "counter >= 60%, prey >= 60%" over 2,000-dr equal-cost pairs; U5a only caps a unit at 62% (`tools/balance.mjs:593`), there is no lower bound.
**Failure scenario.** The table prints "tank beats rifle 65% (n=20)", "AT beats tank 55%", "EMP beats mech 50%". All sit inside the noise, a counter that does nothing passes, and the report claims counters are real. Worse, a unit that is dominated everywhere never fails (no floor).
**Required plan change.** Replace the 20-seed rule by a stated decision rule: each asserted counter pair gets >= 200 battles (about 70 CPU-seconds at 0.33 s/battle) and passes only if the lower 95% Wilson bound is >= 55% and the point estimate >= 65%; each claimed non-counter (a pair that must be near parity) passes only if the interval contains 50%. The counter table is data in `era_<id>/balance.js` with the designed direction, written BEFORE measuring (so measuring cannot define the design). Add a control: the same pair with the mechanic disabled (no armour face, no EMP, no cloak) must lose its advantage, proving the counter is caused by the mechanic and not by raw stats.

### Q9. Ancient campaign regression is deferred to the end, and the fingerprint that should flag staleness does not see half the sim
**Question.** How is an Ancient mission regression found at the commit that causes it, rather than after 12 sim modules?
**Evidence.** Plan G6: "the slow feasibility record (450 battles) is re-run once at the very end". `tests/campaign/feasibility.test.mjs` replays only missions 1 and 8, one seed, and when `b.sim !== now` it only prints "STALE" and still runs. `tests/campaign/_lib.mjs:155-160` `simHash()` reads `readdirSync(src/sim)` and `src/world` with a `.js` filter, which is non-recursive: `src/sim/abilities/*` (27 abilities, plan M13 adds more) are not hashed, and only `era_ancient/stats.js` among content is. Add G6b: after each SIM module, replay all 9 Ancient missions with the authored reference at seed 1 and the 6 puzzle solutions (about 15 battles, under 30 s) and compare end tick, winner and stars with the stored values; this is cheap enough to be part of the fast gate. The 450-battle band run stays at the end and at one mid-point.

Negative controls (Q29) for each rule.

### Q11. Text and safety scans are string lists that Medieval will collide with and Modern will slip past
**Question.** How is "no real nations, wars, extremists, stereotypes, brands" verified, and how is "specific beats generic" verified, for roughly 3,000 new strings?
**Evidence.** `tests/humor/text.test.mjs` H4 sweeps 13 explicitly imported modules for slurs, STEREOTYPE (`crusade`, `crusader`, `savage`, `primitive`, `pagan`...), RELIGION (`church`, `pope`, `bible`...). (4) The blind comedy pass must produce a recorded score file (line id, 1 to 5, reviewer), with a stated threshold (for example mean >= 3.5 and no more than 10% of lines below 3 survive) and the bottom 30% actually deleted; commit the deleted-line list. ### Q12. Copy-in-context bugs (lessons, kill verbs, HUD nouns) were a repeat QA finding and no check is planned

### Q13. The campaign bots verify the designer's own answer, and bands were previously tuned until green
**Question.** What proves a new mission is winnable by a thinking human and not trivially winnable by everyone?
**Evidence.** `tests/campaign/_lib.mjs:34`: `counter` uses `m.reference` (the authored deployment) whenever present; the band "counter 60-100" therefore checks the author's own solution (the lower bound only; 100% passes). Greedy is allowed up to 70% and turtle up to 60%: a mission that a chaos army wins 70% of the time is a free win. The Ancient campaign commit `15f50c0` retuned missions 3, 4, 6, 9 until the stored record was in band (QA2 R2-M2). The harness also carries a "harness-only workaround" for a real sim crash (`trojan_horse` siege without ranged weapon crashes `breachBehaviour`), i.e. the bots run a world the product does not. Plan V4: "campaign win-rate bands (counter 60-100, greedy 25-70, turtle 10-60, per-mission exceptions recorded with reason)" copied from Ancient.
**Failure scenario.** Medieval mission 6 is won by `autoFill` plus FIGHT 68% of the time, which passes (greedy <= 70%); mission 8 star 3 is only reachable by a god-power bot at 1 in 40; the user finds act 3 either trivial or a wall. The bots never touch the mechanics the mission claims to teach.
**Required plan change.** Add per-mission difficulty evidence beyond bands: (a) a `balanced-autofill` no-thought bot must win <= 40% on acts 2 and 3 and <= 60% on act 1; (b) a mechanic-blind variant (reference army with the key mechanic's units replaced by same-cost units without it) must lose >= 70%, which proves the mission demands the mechanic it teaches; (c) win rate is monotone in a "skill ladder" of bots (greedy < balanced < counter < reference); (d) no workaround is allowed in the harness that the product lacks (list any, open a SIM request, fix it before the mission ships); (e) a retune that touches a mission after its record requires re-recording AND a note why in the mission's `botsWhy`, as today.

### Q14. The balance harness cannot exercise the new mechanics, and it judges new roles as lone units
**Question.** How do you know a suppression/cover/EMP/cloak/shield/orbital mechanic is used by the AI and matters in the numbers?

### Q16. Balance has no floor: a useless or dominated unit passes
**Question.** What rejects a unit that nobody should ever buy?
